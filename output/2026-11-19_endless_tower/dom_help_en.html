Expedition?

124 topics

Your first steps

Your goal is to collect good heroes and equipment, improve the party and progress through chapters. Combat runs automatically. Your main decisions are who to bring and which gear, skills and tactics to give them.

After confirming your starting heroes, open Expedition in the left navigation, choose the first stage from its list and press Deploy. Adjust members and formation in Party, and equipment and mastery in Character. Expeditions continue while the game is open even when you are not watching.

Compare newly found gear with equipped items in Character, then try the next stage. When progress slows, revisit cleared areas for equipment, XP and materials, and consider Tavern recruits or Blacksmith crafting and upgrades. Check Construction for the next unlock when a feature is locked. Use progress gained in one activity to try the next stage again.

Departure and parties

Open the stage list with Expedition in the left navigation, then choose a difficulty, chapter and unlocked stage. Inspect enemies and rewards in the departure window, choose a Party preset and press Deploy. Prepare members, formation, potions and tactics in Party.

Each party holds up to 3 heroes, and the current simultaneous expedition limit is 1. A hero cannot join multiple active expeditions.

Rounds and a complete run

A run lasts from entering a stage until that attempt ends. Each fight against a group of enemies is a round. Winning a round grants its rewards and moves on to the next group. Win every round to clear the stage.

After retreat, defeat or closing the game, unfinished rounds keep their enemies and equipment. Rounds already won bring new groups on the next departure. Multiple parties take different groups at the same stage, and unfinished groups return for the next party. Raising Magic Find can improve the same enemies' equipment rarity.

Lost HP does not automatically refill between rounds. Fallen heroes stay out for the rest of that run, so a party that wins early rounds can still fall apart later. This is why healing, protection and killing enemies quickly all matter.

A hero who levels up from a round's rewards starts the next round at full HP. When the run itself ends, everyone recovers regardless of the result and can join the next departure.

Stages and difficulty

Regular areas contain stages I, II and III. Each departure runs one selected stage, with higher-level enemies in later stages of the same area. Stages I and II contain elites; stage III ends with a boss. The last area is a single round against the chapter boss.

On Normal and Nightmare, clearing a stage unlocks the next one, and beating a chapter boss opens the next chapter. Clear records are separate for each difficulty. Once Hell difficulty is unlocked, you can choose the first stage of each available chapter, then clear its stages in order.

Automatic progress and repeat

Expeditions continue while the game is open. A win advances to the next stage; a defeat retries the same stage. A defeat by a chapter boss goes back to the previous stage (and turns off Auto-repeat); winning there heads back to the boss. Turn on [Auto-repeat] to cycle through stages I, II and III of the current area instead of moving to another area. The next departure normally waits 5 seconds; [Skip] in spectate shortens that wait.

With Auto-repeat off, beating the final chapter boss continues from the first stage of the next difficulty, if one exists. Press [Retreat] to stop.

When to advance or repeat

Clearing a new stage can open the next route and meet building requirements. Use [Auto-repeat] when you want more gear, skill books, gold or Codex EXP from the current area. Repeat controls where the party goes next; expeditions continue even with it turned off.

Choose between retrying a difficult stage and farming a manageable area based on what you need. Check the Skill Codex for book sources. For new gear or salvage materials, inspect enemy equipment and keep inventory space available. Materials earned through expeditions can fund further growth at the Blacksmith and Library.

Spectating and changes

Select a Party tab at the top to spectate that party. Combat continues when you view other tabs. Hover heroes or enemies to inspect stats, skills and gear. Click a hero to choose the equip target for the inventory below. Equipment and mastery changes apply immediately to combat in progress, including boss fights.

Automatic departures after wins retain the original preset configuration. To apply changed members, formation, potions or tactics, [Retreat] and [Deploy] again.

Speed, pause and battle information

The ×number buttons in spectate select how quickly that party's battle progresses. Pause stops that party, and Resume continues it. Its chosen speed and paused state persist when you visit another game tab.

The Combat Log shows actions in time order, while Damage compares damage dealt and taken by hero and skill. Press the same button again to close its panel and widen the battle view. Use these panels to see why heroes fall or whether the skills you prepared actually activate.

Loot, reports and recovery

Rewards from won rounds enter that party's inventory and your stock. When its inventory is full, overflow gear is salvaged into dust and slot materials. Overflow can consume gear marked to keep by auto-salvage rules, so leave free slots.

Open Report at the top right of the stage list in Expedition. It shows run history, loot, and each hero's damage dealt, damage taken, kills and falls. Retreating or losing keeps rewards from rounds already won; unfinished rounds grant no rewards.

At the end of a run, lost HP and fallen heroes recover immediately for free. A defeated chapter boss has a respawn wait. No party can enter that boss stage on the same difficulty until it returns. First-clear chests are separate from ordinary kill rewards.

Main missions and chests

The scroll to the right of your gold is the main mission list. Until you beat the Normal Chapter 5 boss it shows the next goal one at a time, and you can claim any completed mission's reward from the panel in any order. Missions do not unlock features or chapters — construction and bosses still do that.

Missions for defeating each area's boss for the first time give a chest. Chests go to the stash, or to the mailbox if the stash is full. Right-click a chest and choose [Use] to get one piece of gear that fits the hero you are viewing, at that hero's level. Clearing a boss stage for the first time no longer grants a separate chest.

Reading a difficult run

Start with the ending reason in [Report]. If a front-line hero fell early, check damage taken and the enemy named in the fall entry, then inspect whether that enemy's skill deals physical or elemental damage. Consider physical defense, the matching resistance, maximum HP or healing. The enemy named in a fall entry is the one that dealt the most damage to that hero.

If heroes survive but the timer expires, check the remaining enemies' HP and damage dealt. Review weapon strength, inactive skills, action speed, skill cooldowns and enemy healing or defenses. Frequent physical misses are a reason to check accuracy and each hero's level deficit.

Change one thing at a time and compare later reports to identify the problem. Enemy groups and equipment can vary within the same area, so a single result does not prove that a new item or formation is always better.

Shrines for the next stage

Continuing from stage I to II, or II to III within the same area, grants a random shrine effect to the whole party for the next stage. Shrines are temporary benefits, not items to click or equip. Cycling from III back to I or moving to another area grants no new shrine.

Shrines can support damage, maximum HP, resistances, life steal, cooldowns, XP or farming. Hover the shrine name during spectate or open [Buffs] to see the current effect. A shrine from the previous stage can explain why a party survives better or defeats enemies faster than usual.

The effect lasts for that one stage attempt and expires when it ends, whether you win or lose. Retrying after a defeat does not keep that shrine. Retreating or closing the game also removes it.

Revisiting chapters on higher difficulties

After Normal, revisit the chapters on Nightmare and then Hell. Difficulty changes enemy levels, HP, damage, equipment and skill loadouts, not just the name. An enemy with the same portrait may need a different response, so inspect its current skills and gear.

For ordinary expedition loot, Epic gear can appear from Nightmare elites and bosses, and Legendary gear from Hell elites and bosses. Normal monsters remain limited to Rare gear even in Hell. More Magic Find does not unlock a rarity unavailable to that difficulty and monster grade.

Hell fixes monster and dropped-equipment levels at the level cap; heroes do not level beyond their cap either. You can choose another chapter's first stage and tackle an area that suits your party and reward needs. Different chapter debuffs make responses beyond leveling important.

The Sin, attribute and defense trait chips shown on elite cards currently have display implementation only. A chip with an immunity name does not yet grant that trait's actual elemental immunity. Judge enemies by their current gear, skills and applied stats.

Hell chapter hazards

On Hell, the departure-window heading shows the chapter's debuff. Its base effect is shared across that chapter, with a stronger effect in the chapter-boss stage. Check it alongside enemy skills when damage rises or healing seems weaker than expected.

Chapter 1, Wrath, increases Physical and elemental direct-hit damage taken from monsters. Chapter 2, Envy, periodically poisons the whole party. Poison Resistance and shorter Poison duration can help in different ways.

Chapter 3, Greed, reduces healing received from healing skills, regeneration, potions and life steal. Chapter 4, Sloth, periodically applies Freeze and slows actions. Freeze does not pause skill cooldowns, so distinguish it from Stun.

Chapter 5, Gluttony, increases monster Physical and elemental direct-hit damage as a round lasts longer, up to a cap. The increase starts over in the next round. Consider whether surviving longer is enough or whether faster kills would reduce time spent facing the stronger effect.

Closing the game

Expedition combat does not continue offline. Closing the game stops parties in combat and parties waiting for their next departure. On return, check the last report, then deploy again to your chosen stage. Exploration and Laboratory offline progress are explained in their own sections.

Map and spots

Click a spot on the map to open its departure, progress or result window. Close it with its close button or an empty area of the map. Beating a chapter boss on Normal opens the next chapter's land. Caves unlock at the first Exploration building rank; caravan and ruins icons appear at later ranks.

Caves currently provide exploration rewards; caravan and ruins activities are coming later.

How exploration differs from expeditions

Current cave exploration returns equipment after a set time. It does not offer spectated round combat or XP like expeditions. The number of heroes sent determines the gear count, so decide whether a preset should fight now or spend time exploring.

A party on an expedition must retreat first. After sending it to explore, you cannot use that preset for expeditions until claiming its reward or recalling it. An existing trip continues while the game is closed, but new trips do not automatically start afterward.

Sending a preset

Choose a preset in the spot window and press [Set out]. A preset with one hero can explore; up to 3 heroes can go together. A party on an expedition must retreat first. A preset cannot depart if a member is already exploring or advancing. Each spot holds one exploration, and the current simultaneous limit is 1.

Time and story

Exploration lasts 1h, including time while the game is closed. The progress bar and remaining time show when it will finish. Story entries open as the trip progresses. Finished rewards wait until you claim them, and the preset and heroes remain busy exploring until then.

Gear rewards and claiming

Caves return gear in proportion to the number of heroes sent (1 per hero), at levels within that chapter's level range. Open the completed spot, inspect the gear and press [Collect]. Rewards enter the currently selected party inventory. Claiming requires enough free slots for the entire reward; make room in the inventory first if needed.

Preset lock and recall

A preset that is exploring cannot change members. Recall the whole preset to change it. Press [Call back] and then confirm; the heroes return immediately with no exploration reward.

Sending an exploring preset on an expedition asks for confirmation first. On confirm, an exploration in progress is called back with no reward, and a finished one is collected into that party's inventory before deploying. If the haul does not fit in the inventory, the party cannot deploy.

Members and leader

Select a Party number, then click heroes in the roster to add or remove them. Clicking a hero on the formation board also removes them. Adding a hero to another preset moves them out of their previous preset. Each party holds up to 3 heroes.

The first hero in the party list is the leader; leadership is separate from the hero's front or back row position.

The leader and Leadership

The first hero in a preset's member list is its leader. The leader's Leadership affects the strength of Party tactics. Standing in the first front-row seat or selecting a hero in Character does not change the leader.

To change the leader, remove every hero from the preset, add the intended leader first, then fill the remaining places. High Leadership does not activate a tactic whose condition is unmet, so check both the condition and the leader's contribution.

What the front row protects

Choose a formation on the left, then drag heroes into front or back seats. Dragging between occupied seats swaps them. Ordinary attacks choose among living front-row targets and reach the back row after the front falls. With no front row, everyone is a target.

A single front-line hero protects the back row but takes all ordinary attacks. More front-line heroes can share those attacks. The better choice depends on their equipment, healing and opposing skills. Skills that hit everyone can still reach the back row.

Rows mainly determine who gets attacked. Heroes can use basic attacks and skills from the back row; melee classes do not lose attacks just for standing there. The leader is the first hero in the party list, separate from formation position or the display order of cards in Character.

Potions shared by the party

The potion belt belongs to the whole party, not to individual heroes. A hero below the HP threshold drinks from the first filled slot. If several heroes need one at once, the lowest HP percentage goes first, with ties following party order. Potions cannot revive fallen heroes.

Click a potion in stock to fill the first empty slot, or click a filled slot to clear it. Drag stock potions to specific slots or drag between slots to change their order. Repeating the same potion in several slots is allowed. Configuring slots does not consume stock.

Each round refills the belt from the departure configuration and current stock. Once its slots are used within a round, no more can be drunk until the next round. A full run can therefore consume more potions than the belt holds. All parties share the same stock; shortages leave slots empty. Potions bought or brewed during a run can replenish the next round.

Choosing when to drink

Use [−]/[+] below the belt to adjust this preset's HP threshold. A living hero drinks automatically when below it, without using an action. The party shares one potion cooldown: after anyone drinks, no one in the party can drink again for 4 seconds.

A higher threshold drinks earlier but can waste healing beyond the missing HP. A lower threshold can make fuller use of each bottle, but risks falling to the next heavy hit. Consider incoming hit sizes and the potion's healing amount. Thresholds are saved per preset. Retreat and deploy again to ensure a change applies to an expedition.

Party tactics and rerolls

A party tactic applies its effect when that slot's condition is met. Use the current values shown with the condition to choose heroes. Higher Command Tent ranks unlock more tactic slots. [Reroll] spends gold to reroll the Sin, effect and grade in unlocked slots. Each slot keeps its own condition. Lock a slot to keep it during rerolls; locking and unlocking are free.

Tactics and locks are stored separately for each preset. Locking more slots raises the cost of rerolling the remaining ones.

Making tactic conditions work

A promising tactic does nothing unless its condition is met by the current preset. A slot that counts heroes of its Sin and one that counts monsters this party has slain together ask for different things. Decide whether to change the party to keep an effect or reroll for a tactic that fits the party you have.

A reroll replaces every open, unlocked slot at once, and keeping more locks makes it more expensive. Before spending heavily to perfect one slot, consider the effects already active and the gold needed for buildings or upgrades. Tactics are not buttons you activate manually during combat.

Changing a preset during an expedition

A preset on an expedition has a locked roster: you cannot add or remove heroes, change its leader or move its heroes to another preset until you retreat. Formation, potions and rolled tactics can still be changed, but they do not replace an active party's departure configuration. Automatic departures after wins keep that configuration; retreat and deploy again to use your changes.

Retries after defeats use the current preset when it can depart. However, changing equipment mid-run changes the gear Sin share of tactics active at departure from that moment. The roster of an exploring preset also stays locked until it returns.

Class, Sin, tier and innate skill

Class determines class mastery and advancement paths; a hero's Sin determines Sin mastery. The innate skill is the active the hero starts with, and heroes of the same class can have different ones. There is no feature to change a hired hero's class, Sin or innate skill.

Normal, Magic and Rare heroes use different attribute-total ranges and distributions. A higher tier offers a higher expected total, not a guarantee that every relevant attribute beats every lower-tier hero. Compare innate skills, actual attributes such as Strength, Agility and Vitality, and the role you want the hero to fill.

A hero's Sin is separate from equipment Sins and from damage elements such as Fire, Cold, Lightning and Poison. A Wrath hero does not automatically deal Fire damage; inspect the skill to see its element. Hero tiers, equipment rarities and monster Normal/Elite grades are also separate classifications.

Choosing heroes and reading stats

Click a hero card to view that hero's equipment, attributes and detailed stats. Drag between roster cards to reorder them. The seven attributes are fixed when the hero is created; equipment and level-ups do not directly raise them. They scale damage, speed, maximum HP, skill effects, leader tactics and Laboratory registration effects.

Hover a stat name to read what it does. Detailed stats show the combat values built by current gear, mastery and other effects.
Strength — Increases basic attack and physical skill damage
Agility — Faster attack and casting speed · More hits and stronger effects for some skills
Intelligence — Increases magic skill damage
Vitality — More Max HP per level-up · Longer-lasting buffs
Luck — Increases Crit Damage, Item Find and Gold Find · Boosts bonus damage of some skills
Leadership — Strengthens the buffs you give allies · As leader, strengthens party tactics
Charisma — Increases healing and the effects you put on enemies · Shortens ailments and debuffs on you

Hero levels and item levels

Hero levels raise maximum HP and grant shared skill points. Skill books, higher mastery tiers and advancement also have level requirements. Leveling alone does not raise the seven attributes or your weapon's attack. Gear supplies the base attack used by basic attacks and many skills.

Lv on an item icon is that item's level, separate from the selected hero's level. The hero must meet its level requirement to equip it. Even at the same item level, rarity, base type, options and upgrade level change its value. Do not replace gear using the Lv number alone. Lv on a skill icon is another value: that skill's growth level.

Expedition XP goes to heroes alive when a round is won. Fallen heroes miss that round's XP, and enemies below a hero's level give less XP. The hero level cap is 50. Beyond it, improve gear, skills, mastery choices and party combinations.

The extra starting goblin

A new game adds one Growth-tier goblin to the roster alongside the heroes you selected. Only the selected starting heroes enter the first preset, so a larger roster does not mean everyone is fighting. Choose who goes in the Party tab.

The goblin starts with low, even attributes and no equipped gear. Check equipment and survivability before placing it in early combat. Despite the Growth tier name, attribute gains on level-up are currently coming later. Levels, HP and skill points still grow as they do for other heroes.

Equipping and comparing gear

Click gear in an inventory or the stash to equip it on the selected hero; click an equipped slot to remove it. The equip restriction is its required level. Any class can manually wear another class's weapon or armor. Being able to equip a weapon does not mean every owned skill works with it.

Hover gear for a side-by-side comparison. [Alt] shows detailed stat changes after equipping; [Shift] shows option roll ranges. For an attacker, compare weapon attack, speed and the skills they actually use. For a front-line hero, also consider HP, physical defense and resistance to the enemy's skill elements.

[Auto-equip] chooses class-suitable gear from the party inventory you are viewing. It does not search for every off-class weapon build you might intend. Options marked (inactive) do not yet affect combat.

Equipment slots and armor types

Equipment slots hold a weapon, helmet, armor, gloves, boots, amulet and two rings. You use one weapon, with no separate shield or off-hand slot. Rings and amulets support combat through their options too.

Armor is not class-locked either. Heavy Armor offers more Physical Defense at a cost to physical attack speed; Light Armor favors physical attack speed, while Robes favor skill cooldown reduction. Choose according to your survival and what makes attacks or skills wait. Physical Attack Speed options do not shorten casting intervals with magic weapons.

Items of the same armor type can differ in item level, upgrades and options. Compare the equipment and resulting stats instead of deciding from its name or type alone.

Rarity color and item level differ

Normal → Magic → Rare → Epic → Legendary are equipment rarities. Higher rarity adds option lines, but the options must still suit your hero. Normal gear has no Sin; Magic has one, and Rare or higher has two different Sins. Items of the same rarity can roll different options and values.

Expedition drops can include Epic gear from Nightmare onward and Legendary gear in Hell. Not every enemy can provide every rarity, so consider difficulty and elite or boss enemies when seeking better gear. Unique and Crafted items are separate categories, not simply the next steps in that ladder.

Item level affects weapon damage, defense and option values, as well as whether a hero can equip it. A higher item level does not add option lines. Compare required weapons and actual stat changes alongside rarity and level.

Equipment options and Sins

Slot, base type, rarity and item level determine equipment base stats and options. Prefixes and suffixes in item names can carry Sins, which Party tactics count on worn gear. A hero's own Sin and the Sins on equipment are separate conditions; check which one the tactic requires.

Party inventories and the stash

Each party has its own inventory; choose a party number to select which one to view and organize. The stash is shared. Use [Ctrl]+click or drag and drop to move gear between an inventory and the stash. [Sort] organizes the current container once by rarity, level or slot; later arrivals append to the end.

When swapping equipment, the old item returns to the container the new one came from. Manually removed equipment enters the currently viewed inventory. Upgrade and craft gear at the Blacksmith.

Salvage and locks

Right-click equipment and choose [Lock] to protect it from salvage or choose [Unlock] to remove that protection. In [Salvage], select gear manually or with rarity, level and base-option filters, then [Salvage now] to receive dust and slot materials. Gear must match every selected filter column; multiple options within the same column match any one of those options.

Salvage permanently consumes items, so lock important gear first. [Auto salvage] sets keep or salvage per rarity for each party. Normal items use [Base] to keep only base options at or above the threshold; Magic and higher use [Word] to keep only items whose sin word in the name is among the higher words possible at that item level (the top word is the original sin name). A level rule can be added too. It checks newly received Expedition drops; use [Apply to inventory now] to process gear already owned. Unique and crafted items are kept by auto-salvage. Save rules to a preset to load them on another party.

Active skills and automatic use order

Active skills come one each from the hero's innate skill, a learned skill book and advancement. Leveling alone does not automatically teach a collection of skills. Hover an icon to check its effect, targets and weapon requirement. Learn and upgrade book skills at the Library.

Every skill is ready when a battle starts, so the first turns use skills in your [Priority] order. [Set Order] fixes it, then drop one skill icon onto another to swap them; [Randomize] clears it. A hero showing “–” instead of numbers has no set order and shuffles it every battle, and monsters cast buffs and debuffs first. After that, combat picks the eligible skill that has been ready longest; skills ready at the same time follow the same order. Remaining cooldowns carry over into the next round of the same stage, and the next stage starts with everything ready again.

Physical weapons and bare hands normally make a basic attack each turn, using a ready skill first. The following basic attack may be skipped if the attack skill's target has already fallen. Magic weapons have no basic attack and cast using stored charges. Channeling prevents other skills and basic attacks while active; auras and event-triggered skills also follow their own activation rules. There is no mana.

Why a learned skill may be inactive

Heroes can learn book skills from another class. However, skills needing a weapon—such as attacks, heals and summons—require a weapon belonging to that skill's class. This applies to innate, learned and advancement skills. Incompatible skills are skipped.

For example, a Warrior can read a Mage book and equip a staff. The Mage attack can then work, while Warrior attacks requiring an axe or mace may become inactive. Skills that only apply effects, such as shouts, blessings or curses, do not depend on the weapon and can be combined on other classes.

Being able to learn a skill, having its required weapon and having useful attributes for it are separate questions. An Archer's physical hit strength also depends on Strength; Agility affects speed and some skills' hit counts or effects. Priest heals use magic attack and Charisma. Self-healing such as Recovery uses maximum HP and Vitality without requiring a weapon. Check each skill's description and [Alt] calculations.

Attack speed, casting speed and cooldowns

Attack Speed and Casting Speed affect the interval between actions. Physical weapons and bare hands use Attack Speed options; magic weapons use Casting Speed. Agility affects both. When detailed stats show the interval in seconds, a lower number is faster.

A cooldown is the wait after using a skill before it is ready again. Cooldown Reduction shortens that wait; attack or casting speed creates action opportunities more often. They do not replace each other. Watch whether a ready skill is waiting for an action, or a hero ready to act is waiting for skill cooldowns.

Magic weapons gain one casting charge when the action gauge fills and store up to 4. Casting a ready skill spends one charge. Several stored charges and ready skills can produce casts in quick succession. Pausing while cooldowns run can build those charges, so waiting and then casting repeatedly is not necessarily a malfunction.

Physical defense and elemental resistance

Physical Defense reduces direct physical hits. Fire, Cold, Lightning and Poison Resistance reduce their matching elemental damage. There is no single magic-defense value covering every element. If a high-defense hero takes heavy Fire damage, check Fire Resistance separately.

Resistance and maximum resistance are different. Once resistance reaches its cap, adding more may not immediately lower damage taken. Maximum-resistance bonuses raise the ceiling; enough actual resistance is still needed to use it. Also consider enemies that reduce resistance.

Damage Reduction, barriers and healing offer other forms of protection, but they do not treat every damage source alike. Some Priest attacks deal Fixed damage and bypass defense, resistance and damage reduction. Poison damage over time and reflection also follow different rules from direct hits.

Defense Penetration, Elemental Penetration and reductions

Defense Penetration leaves part of the enemy's defense out of your own Physical direct-hit calculation. Defense reduction applied by skills lowers the enemy's defense, also helping allies who deal Physical hits to that target. Neither effect improves hit chance.

Elemental Penetration applies to your own elemental direct hits. Options naming Fire, Cold or another element apply only to that element. Resistance reduction applied by skills affects the enemy, so allies attacking with the same element can benefit while it lasts. Reducing Fire Resistance does not also strengthen Physical or Cold attacks.

Gear options give penetration; reductions placed on enemies come from skills. For a party focused on Physical hits, pair Defense Penetration with skills that lower defense. For elemental attacks, match penetration and resistance-lowering skills to the elements actually used. Fixed damage already bypasses defense and resistance, so these options do not raise that Fixed damage.

Direct hits, damage over time, reflection and stagger

Basic attacks and direct skill strikes are direct hits. These can deal Physical, elemental or Fixed damage. Repeated strikes from a channeled skill can each be direct hits, so a sequence of damage numbers does not always mean damage over time.

Poison, periodic aura damage and reflection are not direct hits. They do not trigger further critical hits, life steal or reflection. Poison and aura damage check their matching elemental resistance but bypass barriers, physical defense and ordinary Damage Reduction. Reflected damage is not reduced again by defense, resistance or barriers. A rapid-looking effect does not necessarily provide life-steal healing.

Losing a large amount of actual HP to a Physical or Fixed direct hit can briefly stagger a hero. Stagger delays actions but, unlike Stun, does not pause skill cooldowns. Hit Recovery shortens stagger; it does not restore HP.

Critical hits, Crushing Blow, Life Steal and counters

Critical Chance controls how often critical hits occur; Critical Damage controls their multiplier. Direct hits with magic weapons can also critically hit. Neither stat alone describes the hero's overall damage output.

Crushing Blow adds damage based on the enemy's current HP just before the hit. It uses current rather than maximum HP, so the added amount is larger while that enemy has more HP left. Crushing Blow works with direct hits using Physical weapons or bare hands, but does not apply while wielding a magic weapon.

Life Steal scales with direct-hit damage dealt. Physical damage restores HP, elemental damage becomes a short-lived shield (up to a share of max HP), and Fixed damage provides no Life Steal. Weak hits also mean little healing. Added Crushing Blow damage, Poison damage over time and reflection provide no Life Steal. Compare the Life Steal value with the damage the hero actually deals.

A counter has a chance to answer an incoming hit with a bonus basic attack against the attacker. It does not use the defender's own action turn, and a counter cannot itself be countered. This differs from reflection. Magic weapons have no basic attack and cannot counter; staggered or Stunned heroes cannot counter either.

Item Find, Magic Find, Gold Find and EXP

Item Find raises the chance that a defeated enemy drops gear. Magic Find raises the share of gear rolls that are Rare or better. Despite its name, it does not only favor Magic rarity. It neither raises item level nor unlocks rarities unavailable at that difficulty.

Gold Find increases gold earned from enemies. EXP bonuses on gear increase combat XP for the hero wearing it, not Codex EXP. Compare different options depending on whether you need equipment, funds or levels for a particular hero.

Item Find, Magic Find and Gold Find combine the party's contributions with diminishing returns. Because drops come from gear monsters actually wear, Magic Find can improve enemy equipment too. If higher farming bonuses make clears slower or less reliable, also compare the rewards you actually earn.

Burn, Poison, Freeze and Stun

Burn is not damage over time. It increases incoming Physical and elemental direct-hit damage and reduces healing received. The healing penalty affects healing skills, regeneration, life steal and potions, so a hero who normally survives well may struggle while burning.

Poison deals Poison damage at regular intervals. It can keep lowering HP after an attack ends, and Poison Resistance reduces it. Fire or Poison damage does not automatically apply Burn or Poison; check the effects stated on the skill.

Freeze slows actions while skill cooldowns keep running. Stun pauses both actions and skill cooldowns. Potions, regeneration and effect durations continue during Stun. Heroes standing still can therefore have different causes.

Charisma and matching duration-reduction options can shorten incoming ailments and debuffs. Fire Resistance reduces damage; Burn Duration Reduction shortens an effect. Check which effects persist and which damage sources hurt rather than treating these stats as interchangeable.

Channeling and skills that take time

Channeling skills spread attacks over time or gather power before a single strike. Other skills and basic attacks wait during the channel. Other skill cooldowns keep running, while the channeled skill's own cooldown starts after the channel ends.

Attack speed does not shorten every channel. Skills striking at action intervals and skills charging for a fixed number of seconds use different timing rules. Check hit counts, intervals and charge time in the skill description. Stagger and Stun can delay a channel; being hit does not always cancel it.

Shaping a hero with mastery

[Open Mastery] lets you invest in Sin and Class mastery. Both provide passive effects and share one point pool. They shape the hero's damage, survival or farming strengths rather than adding another equipped active skill.

Click a node for one rank or right-click to refund one rank and point. [Reset Mastery] also refunds mastery investments for free, so you can change allocation to address the problem you face. Check higher-tier level requirements and any matching weapon, armor or equipment-Sin conditions; investing alone does not activate a condition you do not meet.

After advancing at the Library, use the same skill points as mastery to learn and upgrade one main skill. Main ranks unlock at five-level intervals from the advancement unlock level. Refunding it lets you choose another skill within the same path, but the chosen advancement path itself cannot be changed. Special, Variant and Ultimate branches are currently coming later.

Dismissing a hero

Select a hero in Character to show [Dismiss] on their card. Remove all equipment first. Heroes on expeditions, exploring or advancing cannot be dismissed, nor can the last hero in the roster.

Confirming removes the hero from the roster, party presets and Laboratory assignments. This cannot be undone and returns no hiring gold, books or XP. The Growth goblin currently has no dismissal XP potion or rehiring feature either. To keep a hero for later training, remove them from the party rather than dismissing them.

What is a building rank?

A building's rank is a step in the features and slots it unlocks. Building it for the first time gives rank I; further construction adds one rank at a time.

Select a lot on the left to see current features, the next rank and the full rank list on the right. Check what the next rank opens, meet its requirements and cost, then press Build.

The first town unlocks

The first buildings unlock in this order: Tavern → Trading House → Blacksmith → Storage → Command Tent. Each building satisfies the requirement for the next one. Expeditions and claimed commissions provide gold for construction.

The Tavern opens its commission board first; hiring unlocks at a later rank. Building Storage starts the Laboratory's automatic material production. Building the Command Tent opens the first Party tactic slot. Inventory and shared stash spaces can be used before Storage is built.

Requirements and payment

Meet every requirement listed under Next Rank. Depending on the building, these include stage, chapter or difficulty clears, hero levels, total roster levels, and other building ranks. Total roster level uses your highest recorded total, so dismissing a hero does not erase an achieved requirement.

Construction finishes immediately after payment, with no assigned hero or waiting time. Built ranks and their costs cannot be refunded. Check missing materials in the cost display and obtain them through Laboratory production or salvaging gear.

Which building unlocks what?

The Trading House opens purchases; the Tavern opens commissions, hiring and gambling; the Blacksmith opens smithing, upgrades and material combining. The Library opens skill books, advancement and Codex levels.

The Command Tent adds tactic slots. Later Exploration ranks add Parties, while later Storage ranks expand the listed features, including potion slots. Check each building's rank list for exact unlocks.

Building a Laboratory rank differs from researching a Laboratory node. Construction opens new nodes. Research in the Laboratory improves the output, speed and other effects of nodes already open.

Locked and coming features

Click a locked navigation tab to see the building required first. Check Next Rank in Construction for missing requirements and costs. A lock within a feature identifies the building rank that opens it.

A locked feature opens when its construction or progress requirements are met. A feature marked coming later is not available yet. A rank that only opens coming features cannot be built.

Hiring heroes

Once hiring is unlocked, inspect a candidate's attributes and skill, then press Hire. It costs 600G and needs a free roster slot. The hero arrives with a starter weapon and armor.

Hiring adds the hero to your roster. Add them to a Party in the Party tab to fight, or assign them to a Laboratory line for its bonuses. The hired candidate's slot stays empty until the next refresh.

Choosing a recruit

Check the role you need, the innate skill and the attributes that skill uses, alongside the tier color. Normal, Magic and Rare heroes have different attribute-total ranges and spreads. Rare heroes generally have higher totals, but a Magic candidate may roll strongly in the attribute you need.

Attributes are set when a hero is created and are not rerolled by ordinary level-ups. Heroes of the same class can suit different roles because their Sins, attributes and innate skills differ. The current candidate list generates Normal, Magic and Rare heroes, but no Uniques.

Refreshing the recruit list

New candidates (free) replaces the entire recruit list, including candidates still present. Time never changes the list automatically.

Refreshing is always free. After each refresh, wait 30 min before refreshing again. The button is disabled during the cooldown and shows the remaining time beside it. The cooldown continues offline.

Heroes who arrive over time

Each hero assigned to the Laboratory's Search line brings back one candidate per interval. With no one assigned, nobody arrives and heroes come only from the hiring candidates. Assigned heroes can still join expeditions and formations, and time keeps passing while the game is closed. These arrivals can be Normal, Magic or Rare heroes. If there is no Growth goblin in your roster or Waiting Room, a goblin sometimes arrives instead.

Arrivals stay until hired or sent away, in the slots to the right of the searcher who brought them. While that searcher's slots are full, the searcher rests at the Tavern instead of searching and sets out again as soon as you free a slot. If the row fills up during a search, the hero found waits outside for room and enters as soon as you free a slot there. A waiting hero shows as Found! under the searcher who brought them; click it to hire them right away or pass. Laboratory Interval research makes arrivals more frequent, Quality raises the Rare chance, and the Waiting Room opens a second slot in each row and then Filter. Set a minimum grade with Filter and lower-grade heroes are turned away while the searcher sets out again at once.

Adding a commission to your expeditions

Check a card's goal and gold reward, then Accept. Your active limit is 1. No extra Party or battle starts: won rounds from all your expeditions contribute after acceptance.

Hunt and Bounty goals require the stated chapter, race or place, and defeated rounds do not count. Salvage goals count gear you salvage by hand or by auto-salvage rules, but not full-bag overflow. Gathering goals count ore, herbs and timber from salvage and the Laboratory, not Shop purchases, trades or synthesis. Enhance goals count failed attempts too. Skill book goals count each book you learn or level up, and hiring goals count each hero you hire. Gear collection goals count gear from won rounds, not from the Shop, chests or Explorations. Nothing from before acceptance counts.

Use Claim after completion to receive the gold. There is no deadline. Abandon loses progress without a fee. Claiming or abandoning replaces that card. A goal matching your current farming area or next enemies can progress alongside your expeditions.

Card grade and Fame level

Each commission rolls its own Normal, Magic or Rare grade. Higher grades increase both the goal and reward, with more gold per unit of the goal.

Fame is shared commission progress for your whole roster. Claiming completed commissions earns XP. Higher Fame levels increase the goals and gold on new cards, and every card on the board you have not accepted is rerolled at the new level. Accepted commissions keep their progress.

Fame never falls, and abandoning grants no XP. At the highest Fame level, you can continue accepting its commissions and earning rewards.

Gambling Den slots

Open the Tavern's Gambling Den, choose a stake tier and spend gold to spin. There is no spin limit, but each spin costs a stake. Stakes follow your progress chapter and chosen tier. Spin ×10 stops early if you cannot afford the next stake.

Three matching symbols on any of 5 rows or diagonals pay gold or materials. Wilds substitute for other symbols; coins break ordinary lines. At least 3 coins starts Hold & Spin. Coins stick in place, and each new coin resets remaining spins to 3. Fill the board for the Grand reward.

Prizes are gold and the shown materials, with no equipment or stigma. Every provided stake tier is available when the hall opens. The Laboratory's Gambling Den line is a separate source of automatic gold income.

Compare, then buy

Choose a comparison hero in the top strip. Hover an equipment item to compare it with that hero's worn item. Alongside level and rarity, check whether its options suit the skills and Party tactics you use.

Select an equipment item, then press Buy below. It enters the party inventory you are viewing and is not equipped automatically. Buying equipment requires enough gold and a free inventory slot.

Skill books enter book stock, and potions enter shared potion stock. Neither occupies equipment slots, so a full equipment inventory does not block these purchases.

Each equipment or skill-book slot can be bought once per list. Sold slots remain until Refresh.

In the Shop, clicking equipment in the inventory or stash below adds it to the sale list. Click it again to deselect it. Only pressing Sell removes the equipment and credits gold. Sale prices are one third of the shop price for the same level and rarity, rounded down. Locked equipment and unopened boxes cannot be sold. Use Salvage in the inventory when you need materials.

Locked slots are goods not yet on sale. A newly built Trading House sells only the top row of weapons and potions; upgrade it in Construction to open the armor, jewelry and skill-book rows.

Check the hero level before refreshing

Refresh regenerates gear around the selected hero's current level. Selecting another hero or leveling up does not change existing goods. Products may roll above or below the target level, and weapons are not restricted to that hero's class.

Refreshing is always free. After each refresh, wait 1h before refreshing again. The button is disabled during the cooldown and shows the remaining time beside it. The cooldown continues offline.

Refreshing changes both gear and skill books and refills sold slots. Unsold goods also disappear, so check intended purchases first. Time and special-trader visits never refresh the ordinary list automatically.

Buying potions in batches

Select a potion, choose a quantity to check its total price, then press Buy. Quantity buttons only select an amount; they do not purchase it immediately. Click the selected quantity again to return to one.

Potions never sell out and accumulate in shared stock. If you cannot afford the total, none are purchased. Configure potion types and order in Party to use them in combat. Shop purchases cost gold; brewing at the Blacksmith costs gold and herbs. Compare the prices shown in each place.

After buying a skill book

The skill icons below the potions in the Shop are skill books. Bought books enter book stock; select a hero under Skill Upgrade in the Library to learn or upgrade it. Extra copies of the same book are used for upgrades.

The ordinary Trading House currently sells Tier 1 class-skill books. It does not stock every skill. Check a skill's Codex entry for other sources.

Buy with materials or gold

Build Trading House rank 2 to open the Trading Post. First select the lowest-grade material to receive. Other materials show their required and owned amounts, and a gold option shows its price. Press Buy on a payment row to spend 3 of that material and receive 1 of your selection.

Trade lowest-grade iron ore, herbs, timber and dust. Selecting a material spends nothing. Choose a material payment or gold. Only the selected currency is spent, and you must have enough of it. Gold prices vary by material, with herbs priced higher. Purchases have no timer or stock limit.

Each time a visiting trader arrives, the material they deal in goes on Deal. Buy it for 2 of another material or 70% of its gold price. The deal lasts until the next trader arrives, and the title bar shows the current and next deal. Make higher grades by combining materials at the Smithy.

Trading House rank 3 opens armor, jewelry and skill-book sales.

Special visits are separate

Upgrading the Trading House to the required rank opens special-trader visits. Its panel shows the time until arrival or departure. This timer is separate from the ordinary Trading House's free Refresh wait.

Special-trader goods are currently coming later. Question-mark slots cannot be purchased. Buy ordinary gear, potions and skill books from the regular Trading House on the left.

Smithing, upgrades and combining

Smith spends materials to make new gear. Upgrade improves the base performance of a weapon or armor you already own. Combine turns several materials into the next material grade.

Check Smith when you need a new gear type, or Upgrade to improve gear you already use. Combining does not alter equipment or reroll its options.

Smithing new gear

In Smith, choose an equipment slot and an unlocked level. Find the gear type you want, then press Make on that row. The result enters the party inventory you are viewing, so it needs a free slot.

Each craft produces one item of the selected type and level, with newly rolled rarity and options. Repeated crafts do not guarantee identical options. Current smithing consumes gold, the slot's material and dust, without consuming existing equipment.

Weapons and jewelry use iron ore; armor pieces use timber. Blacksmith ranks unlock higher smithing levels. Check the row's cost display for exact materials and amounts.

Brewing and stocking potions

Select the potion icon in Smith. Check healing, owned stock, and gold and herb costs, then use Make to add one potion. Brewing cannot fail and does not roll random options.

Blacksmith ranks unlock brewable tiers. All Parties share stock. Filling preset slots does not spend it immediately; drinking in combat does. Check supplies when running repeated expeditions. Set potion types, order and drinking HP threshold in Party.

Upgrading owned gear

Click worn gear on the left, or gear in inventory or stash below, to select a target. Clicking gear here selects it rather than equipping it. In Upgrade, check the next effect, success chance, and gold and material costs.

Upgrades raise base performance, such as weapon damage or armor defense. They do not change the existing Sins, option combination or rolled option values. Amulets and rings cannot be upgraded. Blacksmith ranks can raise the cap.

Success adds one upgrade level. Failure still spends gold and materials. At higher levels a failure can drop the item one level or break the item; a broken item leaves the same materials as salvaging it. Both worn and stashed gear can be upgraded.

Combining the next material grade

Combine uses the immediately lower grade of the same material and gold to make the next grade. For example, combine lowest-grade iron ore into low-grade iron ore. It does not exchange one material type for another, such as ore for herbs.

Combine makes one; Max makes as many as your current materials and gold allow. Combining is immediate and cannot fail. Inputs are consumed and cannot be converted back to lower grades.

Smithing, upgrades, potions, skill books and research currently consume lowest-grade materials. Uses for higher grades are still coming later. Keep the lowest-grade materials you currently need before combining your stock.

Current Craft availability

Craft is currently coming later. Available operations are Smith, Upgrade and Combine.

Books and advancement

A skill book is consumed by a hero to learn or improve a skill. Follow get a book → choose hero → learn or upgrade.

Advancement is a separate choice of growth path for a hero. The learned-skill slot filled by a book and the Advance-skill slot are different. Advancing does not remove the hero's innate or book-learned skill.

Getting and salvaging books

Skill books come from defeating monsters on Expeditions, Exploration cave rewards, the Laboratory Library line, Trading House purchases and each hero's first-book level reward. Check a skill's Codex sources to see which monsters drop its book.

Books enter book stock immediately and do not take equipment inventory slots. Select a book you no longer need in the stash's Skill Books tab and press Salvage. Each press turns one book into 1 skill shard(s) and cannot be undone.

The Laboratory Library line produces skill shards every hour, even with no hero assigned. Assign a hero to that line and each shard has a Jackpot chance to also bring one book of a skill that hero has.

Crafting books from skill shards

Books for every class skill can be crafted in the Library's Skill Book Crafting. Select an icon, check the cost and press Craft to add one copy to your book stock.

A Tier 1 book costs 10 skill shards and a Tier 2 book 20 skill shards; every book also costs 500G. Books of monster or chapter-boss innate skills and advancement skills cannot be crafted.

Choose a hero, then learn

Choose the hero who will read the book in the top strip, then select an owned book in Skill Upgrade. Heroes can use books from level 10. Learn spends one book to fill that hero's learned-skill slot. Teaching another hero requires another copy.

If the hero already learned a different skill, the button becomes Overwrite and asks for confirmation. Confirming removes the old skill and its upgrades without returning used books. Check the new skill's effect and weapon requirement first.

A hero can read another class's book, but weapon-powered attack, healing and summoning skills require a weapon belonging to that skill's class. Learning a skill does not guarantee that current equipment can cast it.

Using matching books to raise a skill level

Choose a book matching the selected hero's innate or already-learned skill to upgrade that skill instead of learning a new one. This does not add another active slot.

Spend the matching copies shown on the button to add one skill level. Higher steps require more books; reading costs nothing else. The upgrade affects that skill on the selected hero. Advance main skills use shared skill points and require the corresponding hero level.

Choosing an advancement path

Select a hero in the Library and press Enter on the Chamber of Advancement panel. Each of the three paths has its own row with its role and the effects of its three advancement skills. The top path is selected when the chamber opens, with its mastery board attached on the right. Click another path to switch the selection and the board. Click an advancement skill on the board to show its strengthening cells in the row below. Hover a cell to see its effect. Previewing spends no resources or points.

Unlock advancement and reach level 30, then select a path and press Advance. The cost is 50,000G and advancement currently completes instantly. The path cannot be changed. Heroes can advance during Expeditions; those exploring must return first.

After completion, use Open Mastery at the bottom of the chamber to learn and improve an advancement skill. Advancement does not automatically teach a skill. Skills can be swapped within the chosen path.

Shared skill points and the Advancement Tree

Heroes earn skill points at every level, starting at Lv1. Sin mastery, Class mastery and the Advancement Tree share this pool.

After advancing, learn one of the path's three skills — it costs no skill points. The tree has two rows. The common row affects the whole path; the skill row (Power · Variant 1 · Variant 2) affects the learned skill. Split up to 5 ranks per row freely across its three cells; putting all 5 into one cell turns on its bonus.

Right-click a cell to refund one rank, or the learned skill to refund its whole skill row to the shared skill-point pool. Click another advancement skill to swap to it — the skill row is refunded and the common row stays. The chosen path and gold cost remain.

Lines, nodes and ranks

A Laboratory line is a field of automatic output: Resources, Search, Training, Gambling Den or Library. A node is research that improves a line's output, interval, quality or another property.

Select a line card on the left to see output and hero bonuses on the right. Select a round node for its current value, next effect, cost and research time. The hero strip at the top shows assignments; open an actual assignment from a line's portrait or +.

Locked nodes need the shown building or rank first. Raising the Laboratory rank in Construction opens new nodes and raises how far each node can be researched. Use Research here to raise their levels.

Upgrade the Library in Construction to open the Laboratory Library line, then assign heroes to it. Each assigned hero produces books of a randomly chosen innate or learned basic skill. Advance skills are excluded; reaching a skill upgrade cap does not stop production — salvage spare books in the stash.

Automatic output and time away

Building Storage opens the Laboratory's Resources line. It produces lowest-grade iron ore, herbs and timber together without assigned heroes. Assignment improves output; it is not required to start production.

Mining, Gathering and Logging research raise ore, herb and timber output respectively. Check ore for weapons or buildings, timber for armor or buildings, and herbs for potions, books or research with an herb cost. Output enters stock automatically.

Production continues while the game is open. Offline materials, Training and Gambling Den income are limited using a 3h calculation window; time away does not accumulate without limit. The Search arrival timer has no such time cap and continues until its Waiting Room fills.

Where materials come from and go

Iron ore comes from Laboratory Mining and weapon salvage. It funds weapon and jewelry smithing, weapon upgrades and construction. Timber comes from Logging and armor salvage, and funds armor smithing, upgrades and construction.

Herbs come from Gathering and salvaging rings or amulets. They fund potions, skill books and research that lists an herb cost. Dust comes from salvaged gear and funds smithing, equipment upgrades and skill books. Gambling can also award the materials shown on its paytable.

Expedition enemies do not directly drop these materials. Obtain them by salvaging the gear they drop. Material stock is shared; view quantities in the stash's Materials tab.

Distinguish type from grade. Ore, timber, herbs and dust are types. Lowest, low, mid, high and top are grades. Current costs use lowest-grade stock; make higher grades with Combine at the Blacksmith. Owning a different grade does not satisfy a cost.

Starting, finishing and canceling research

Select a node and compare its current and next-level values. Check the cost and time, then press Research to pay gold and the listed materials up front. The entire Laboratory can run one project at a time.

Research finishes at its scheduled time even while the game is closed; its new effect applies after completion. No next project is queued automatically. Existing production continues during research.

Use Cancel before completion to recover the full cost and lose that project's progress. Completed levels cannot be refunded this way. Assigning heroes to that line first affects the cost and duration calculated when research starts.

Hero assignment and the two slot types

Select a line's hero portrait or + to open assignments. Select the heroes for this line and press [Apply]. Click again to deselect; selecting a hero assigned elsewhere moves them to this line. Moving or removing a hero restarts their Mastery and Search clock, shown in red at the bottom of the window. Each hero can be assigned to only one line at a time.

Assignment does not occupy the hero. They can stay assigned while joining a Party, fighting Expeditions, exploring or advancing. Training XP, however, goes only to heroes assigned to Training.

Seats hold your heroes who boost a Laboratory line, and every line has the same number of seats. Waiting Room slots hold candidate heroes awaiting hire in the Tavern. To keep more arriving candidates, research the Waiting Room node on the Search line.

Choosing heroes to assign

On the Resources and Gambling Den lines each assigned hero adds one more share of the base output — fill the seats before comparing stats. On Search each assigned hero runs a clock of their own and brings someone in on it. Training and the Library give XP or books to each assigned hero, so they have no head share.

Strength adds a bonus to that line's production, arrival or training speed. Luck raises extra-output chance on lines with a Jackpot node. Intelligence shortens that line's research time; Agility lowers research costs. Charisma raises Search's Rare-candidate chance.

Vitality strengthens the proficiency bonus that builds while a hero remains assigned to the same line. Leadership boosts other assigned heroes on that line, so it matters with multiple heroes. These effects use the hero's base attributes rather than equipment or level.

Select the line card to check current hero bonuses. Effects do not apply to lines that have no use for them. Moving or removing a hero resets the time accumulated on that assignment, so compare heroes before settling their positions.

Search, Training and automatic gold

Search brings one candidate to the Tavern per interval for each hero assigned to this line, and does nothing with no one assigned. Interval makes arrivals faster. While that searcher's Tavern slots are full, the searcher rests and sets out again as soon as you free one of them. Quality raises Rare chance, Waiting Room opens a second slot per searcher and then Filter, and Roster raises how many heroes you can keep.

Training helps assigned heroes catch up toward the roster's leading level. Its cap is the highest roster level minus the gap shown by Range. Research Range to reduce that gap. Heroes at or above the cap gain no Training XP, so Expeditions must push the leading level forward.

The Gambling Den line automatically produces gold once the Tavern's Gambling Den is unlocked. It needs no stake or spin button. Income raises output, while Jackpot raises the chance of an extra payout. This is separate from manually playing Tavern slots.

Facing monsters in the Endless Tower

The Endless Tower lets you climb floor by floor against monster packs. Each floor replays an elite or boss round from the Expeditions, so you can see how far your current party holds. It is separate from chapter progress, and heroes out on an Expedition can challenge it without leaving that Expedition.

Choose a Party, fill all 3 hero slots and select an open floor. The floor's three monsters stand on the board; hover a card to see its gear and skills. Press Challenge to start one automatic battle.

How the floors are built

There are 50 floors, ten per chapter starting from Chapter 1. Each stage has two floors — first an elite floor with one elite and two normal monsters, then a boss floor with that stage's boss and 2 escorts. Stage 5 boss floors bring the chapter boss with its fixed elite guards.

The floor number is the enemy level, and from floor 31 the Nightmare rules apply, making monsters stronger. The same floor always fields the same monster pack. Only the first floor is open at the start; beat the highest open floor to unlock the next. Cleared floors can be retried, and losing never removes unlocked floors.

Endless Tower rules

Battles follow Expedition rules for a single round. Your heroes' attributes, gear and skills plus the selected Party's tactics, potions, formation and Codex bonuses all apply, and the monsters keep their Expedition traits such as family buffs. If you cannot defeat every monster before the time limit, the result is a draw and the next floor stays locked.

There are no rewards — no XP, gold, equipment, skill books or Codex EXP, and commissions and main missions do not progress. Potions are filled from your stock, but drinking them does not reduce it. Only the floors you have climbed are saved.

The result is decided by the heroes, gear and Party setup at the moment you press Challenge; spectating replays that battle.

Testing your party in the Tower of Trials

The Tower of Trials lets you challenge fixed hero teams one floor at a time. It is a place to test how the equipment and formations you built during Expeditions perform against another hero team. It is separate from chapter progress, so you can challenge it whenever you choose.

Choose a Party, fill all 3 hero slots and select an open floor. Edit party takes you to the Party tab. Prepare members, front/back rows, potions and tactics there, then return to the Tower. Inspect the enemy cards for gear and skills, and press Challenge to start one automatic battle.

Climbing floors

Only the first floor is initially open. Beat the highest open floor to unlock the next; cleared floors remain available to retry. Each floor uses a fixed AI team. The floor number is the enemy level, and higher floors also bring stronger gear and skills.

Losing does not remove unlocked floors.

When a floor stops your progress

A floor keeps the same opposing lineup. Watch who falls first and which enemy skills deal the most damage, then change equipment or formation and try again. Changing one thing at a time makes it easier to compare what helped.

If Challenge is disabled because hero slots are empty, fill the selected Party with 3 heroes. Check the tactics and potions shown below your team. You can return to Expeditions, hiring or crafting to improve the party; cleared floors remain when you come back.

Rules shared by both duel modes

Tower of Trials and Arena duels use your heroes' attributes, gear and skills plus the selected Party's tactics, potions and Codex bonuses for one battle. Codex bonuses apply just as in Expeditions. Opponents use their own party tactics and potions under the same rules. Reaching the time limit ends in a draw.

Potions are filled from the stock you own, but drinking them in a duel does not reduce that stock. Duels cost no gold or materials and grant no equipment or Codex EXP. Tower floors, and Arena rating and records, track your progress in each mode.

The result is determined using the heroes, gear and Party configuration present when you press Challenge or Start duel. Spectating replays that battle. Later equipment or formation changes apply to the next duel, and leaving the replay keeps the result already recorded.

Understanding rating and leagues

The Arena tracks rating: a duel score that changes with wins, draws and losses. The Tower of Trials opens floors in order, while the Arena lets you choose opponents near your score. Opponents are defense parties registered by other players; if few players are near your score, the list is short or empty.

Fight with the 3 heroes in your chosen Party. Wins raise rating and losses lower it. Beating a higher-rated opponent gains more, while losing to one costs less. A draw can also change rating depending on the difference between your scores. For example, drawing against a higher-rated opponent can raise your rating.

A league is the name of a rating range. Check the league table on the left for the next threshold; crossing it changes your displayed league. Rating and leagues track progress in this mode and do not directly raise hero levels or Expedition equipment.

Choosing an opponent

The candidates on the left are below, near and above your rating. A card's + shows the points gained for a win, and − shows the points lost for a defeat. Lower-rated opponents offer smaller winning gains; higher-rated opponents offer larger ones. Also inspect their classes and skills to judge how your party matches up.

Select a candidate to reveal that team on the right. Check your Party and the potions and tactics below it, then press Start duel. New opponents refreshes the candidate list without starting a fight. Candidates also refresh after a rated duel. The result and rating are recorded when the fight starts, so leaving the replay keeps the changed score.

Defense party and defense log

The first time you enter the Arena, register a name and a defense party. Only a Party with 3 heroes can be chosen, and pressing Register lists it right away in other players' opponents and in Fight a friend. A four-digit tag fixed to your account follows your name so players with the same name can be told apart. You can change the name in Settings, but it cannot be left empty.

Switch your defense to another Party under Defense party on the left; it cannot be unregistered. Changing its gear or formation uploads the new version, and your rating is shown with it. The party you attack with is the one chosen on the right, and it may be the same as your defense party.

When another player fights your defense party, the Defense log records when, who, their rating and the result, marked Friendly for friendly duels. Defense results do not change your rating. Registering and receiving work only on the live build; opponents and records are refreshed when the game starts, when you enter the Arena and when you press New opponents. If they were just received, the stored list is used. Other players' teams appear only if they pass the rule check, and they use the party tactics and potions of the uploaded party.

Fight a friend

Press Fight a friend to list players who registered a defense party, most recently updated first. Type the start of a name to find names beginning with it, a tag such as #1234 to find that tag, or Name#1234 to find that player. Names are case-sensitive. Results come one page at a time; press More to continue.

Pick a player to close the window and show their team as your friendly opponent. Inspect that team, then press Start duel. The team is as that player last uploaded it, including its party tactics and potions.

Friendly duels are practice. Results do not affect rating or rated records; the other player's defense log shows them marked Friendly.

Records and Expeditions

Battle preparation, potions and Codex bonuses follow the shared rules in the Tower of Trials section. A duel does not advance Expedition stages or Codex EXP, and the Arena has no separate equipment or gold rewards.

Current rating, best rating and rated wins, draws and losses are saved automatically. Selected opponents and the last-battle display reset when you return. A saved record does not mean the previous replay remains available.

Inspecting monsters and Codex EXP

Choose a chapter under Monsters to inspect each area's monsters, classifications, skills and Codex progress. You can preview areas you have not yet unlocked in Expeditions. Hover a monster card for its story and tier rewards, or a skill icon for its effect. The Normal/Elite selector changes the displayed portrait; it does not split that monster's Codex EXP into separate tracks.

Codex EXP is a separate record for each monster, apart from the XP that levels up heroes. It is awarded when kills from a won Expedition round are settled. Kills from lost rounds or rounds abandoned partway through do not count. Codex EXP per kill is Normal 1 · Nightmare 2 · Hell 4, multiplied for Elites and bosses (Elite ×3 · Stage Boss ×10 · Chapter Boss ×20).

Reading race and class

Monster cards identify race and class. Races are Natural, Demon and Undead. Class describes the monster's skills and equipment, such as Warrior, Archer, Knight, Mage or Priest. For example, a Goblin Scout is a Demon with the Archer class.

Codex Lv1–40 bonuses (the farming and combat tiers) are shared within a race. Leveling one Demon monster also helps against Demon monsters in other areas. Monsters with no bonuses in these two tiers, such as bosses, neither grant nor receive those race bonuses.

How race changes a monster's body

Regular and Elite monsters have body traits determined by race. Even monsters with the same class can differ in maximum HP, regeneration, damage, defense and resistance, so inspect their combat values together with their skills. These racial body adjustments do not apply to bosses or hero teams in either duel mode.

Natural monsters have higher physical defense and lower elemental resistances. If physical attacks are struggling, inspect heroes and skills that deal elemental damage.

Demons have higher maximum HP and HP regeneration, lower physical defense, and are immune to Freeze. If their HP keeps recovering during a long fight, consider physical damage and effects that reduce healing received.

Undead have lower maximum HP, deal more damage, and are immune to Crushing Blow and Life Steal. Prepare the damage to defeat them quickly, protection such as defense or damage reduction, and healing in place of Life Steal.

What Codex milestones grant

Each monster's Codex level rises from Lv1 to Lv60 in three tiers of 20 levels. The cumulative Codex EXP at the end of each tier is Lv.1 ~ 20 50,000 · Lv.21 ~ 40 200,000 · Lv.41 ~ 60 1,000,000. Each level within a tier adds 1/20 of that tier's effect, and a full tier grants the whole value shown on the card. Levels never go down.

Lv1–20 is a farming option that improves rewards such as XP, drops or gold from that race. Lv21–40 is a combat option used against the same race. The option varies by monster, and bonuses built from different members of one race combine. Bosses and some other monsters have no bonuses in these two tiers.

Lv41–60 permanently adds one combat option from that monster to every party member. Inspect the monster card for each tier's effect and how many levels you have filled. Codex bonuses do not apply in Tower of Trials or Arena duels.

When a full milestone is still locked

Codex EXP and milestone access are two requirements. You need both the monster's required EXP and the Library rank that opens that milestone before its effect applies. EXP keeps accumulating behind locked milestones, and their markers can fill in grey.

If you already have the EXP for the next milestone but it still shows a lock, hover its marker to see the required Library rank. Build that rank in Construction to apply the milestone already earned. You do not need to collect the same EXP again.

Characters and items

Characters collects hero portraits by class, and Items lists weapon, armor and potion artwork. Hover potions to see their effects. The portrait style selector also changes portraits and backgrounds used in the game and shares its saved choice with Settings. Viewing artwork in the Codex does not grant heroes or equipment.

Finding skills and mastery to learn

The Skills list groups regular and advancement skills by class and advancement. Hover an icon to read its effect and weapon requirement. Regular skills that can become books also show sources and what improves per level. To pursue a skill, find where its book comes from here.

The Codex lets you preview skills and mastery. Learn books at the Library, and spend skill points through Open Mastery in Character. Codex skill details have no selected hero context, so check the skill on your actual hero for their damage or healing values. The Special, Variant and Ultimate advancement branches are currently coming later.

Previewing effects

Under Effects, select a card to play that skill's battle effect. A group's ▶ plays that group; Play all plays every effect. Compare art styles at the top. Codex previews play even when skill effects are disabled in Settings. Choosing a preview style here does not change the live battle style in Settings.

Finding the explanation you need

Help folders follow the same order as the left menu. Select a folder to open only that topic, then scroll inside its text to read further. Each folder remembers your reading position. You can also read about tabs that are still locked.

Enter words separated by spaces in Search to find explanations across all folders whose titles, subheadings and text contain every word. Choose a folder with matches to read them. If the current folder has no matches, the first matching folder opens. Clear the search to restore every folder's explanations.

If you are new, start with Your first steps and Common terms. If something has stopped your progress, use Where to look when stuck to find the relevant tab, then read its detailed explanation.

Common terms

Your roster is every hero you own. A Party is a group from that roster prepared to deploy together; the Party number selects that saved group. A hero can belong to only one Party. Keeping the full roster separate from the deployed Party makes hiring and team preparation easier to follow.

Farming means repeating accessible content to collect wanted equipment, materials or XP. An item's options are its individual effects, such as damage, resistance or healing. Rarity means grades such as Normal, Magic and Rare for gear or heroes; it is separate from level.

A building's rank unlocks features and raises limits. A Laboratory node's level improves that node's production or effect. Mastery provides passive effects by spending points and is prepared separately from active skills learned through books.

Mouse, keyboard and touch controls

Hover hero, monster, equipment or skill artwork to open its information card, called a tooltip. Right-click equipment to choose the available actions, such as locking, moving or salvaging it. Hold Alt for detailed stats, skill calculations or equipment stat changes, and Shift for the possible value ranges of equipment options.

On touchscreens, hold an image to open its information. Details and Range buttons, when shown below the card, provide the Alt and Shift views. Available actions such as equipping or moving appear there too. Move your finger to scroll, or keep it still while holding to open information.

Close most windows with ×, an outside click or Esc. Windows requiring a choice use their own buttons. On mobile, use the bottom icons to switch screens and Menu to reach every tab. In landscape, the icons move to the left. Tap a party status at the top to return to combat. The button at the bottom right of the battlefield shows or hides the bags.

Where to look when stuck

If you cannot deploy, check that the Party contains heroes, that they are not away on another Expedition or Exploration, and that the selected stage is open. The displayed rejection reason tells you which requirement to solve first.

If you can deploy but keep losing, inspect Report and spectate to see who fell first and what dealt damage. Check equipped gear and unspent skill points in Character, then formation, potions and tactic conditions in Party. Expedition and Character help give more detailed examples for each problem.

For locked buttons or tabs, check the building and next-rank requirements in Construction. For materials, check Laboratory production and gear salvage. For gold, run Expeditions or claim completed commissions. Match the material type and grade to the cost shown. A filled but locked Codex milestone needs Library ranks. Start with the requirements for the feature you need now.

Display settings and language

Use the language button at the top to switch between Korean and English. On mobile, it is inside Menu.

If combat visuals feel busy, adjust skill effects, basic attack effects, hit reactions and attack movement separately. A higher transparency value makes skill artwork fainter. Turning Skill Effects Off dims its style and transparency controls; turning Hit Reactions Off dims Shake on Hit. Turning them back on restores the previous choices.

Grid/Text chooses whether battle log entries are divided into columns or read as sentences. Language, visual effects and log settings do not change battle outcomes or equipment effects, and are saved in this browser.

New game and starting heroes

Choose New Game from the menu to inspect the classes, Sins and attributes of your 3 starting heroes. Reroll replaces every candidate and can be used without limit until you find a team you like. Confirming starts the prologue with those heroes as your first party, each carrying a weapon suited to their class.

If a save already exists, press the overwrite button again to confirm a new game. Back returns to the menu, where you can load the existing save.

Autosave and continuing

Progress is saved automatically in this browser. When you reopen the game, continue with Load Game from the menu. Saves belong to the browser and site address, so another browser, device or address does not automatically continue the same save. Clearing this site's browser storage also clears the autosave.

If multiple game tabs are open in one browser, a tab that saves causes the older tab to show a paused screen. Choose one tab to play in. Continue in this tab reloads the page to read the latest save; if the menu appears, use Load Game to continue.

Rerolling candidates in New Game or returning with Back keeps the existing save. Final confirmation to start with new heroes replaces the autosave with the new game. Display settings and language are stored in this browser separately from game progress.

Viewing other screens or stepping away

Expeditions and Exploration continue while you view other in-game tabs such as Help, Character or Construction. Activity is not limited to the team you are spectating, so you can organize equipment while combat runs.

Closing the game stops Expeditions; deploy again after returning. An exploration already sent can finish while the game is closed; claim it in Exploration when you return. Offline Laboratory production, Training and income use a limited 3h calculation window. Search continues until its Waiting Room fills.

If the PC sleeps or the browser suspends the game tab for a long time, Expeditions can stop. On return, check the last report, then Deploy again. Check Laboratory output and Exploration completion or collection in their respective tabs.

Item generator

The Lab is an item inspection tool available only in development environments. Choose a chapter and monster, then adjust Normal/Elite grade, difficulty and stage level. Changing monsters resets the level to that area's default. Bosses keep their own fixed grade.

Drop conditions and rolls

The slot row distinguishes always-equipped items from optional slots with a wear chance. Item-level ranges and rarity weights show the actual drop rules for your chosen conditions. Roll generates once; ×10 generates ten times. Party Magic Find is excluded. Weights are relative shares, not direct percentage chances.

Results and saving

The right side shows the latest item's details and recent results. Hover a result to inspect it and press Clear to erase the list. Generated equipment is not added to inventory and does not affect real gold, materials or saves. The list resets when you reload.

Raid · Three parties, one encounter

The left, center and right parties fight one shared boss. Defeat that chapter’s Hell boss to try your own parties. Each needs three distinct heroes and must be free of expeditions and exploration.

Equipment, skills, formation and tactics are copied at deployment. Healing, buffs and auras stay within their party. Debuffs on the shared boss benefit everyone. Surviving parties keep fighting if another falls.

Raid · Warnings and cast breaks

Warnings show the attack, targeted parties and time to impact, with matching lane borders. Hammer prioritizes the targeted front rank. Cleaves and waves hit everyone in their targeted lanes. Marks and taunts apply within the selected lane.

Dealing real HP damage fills a heavy attack’s cast-break bar. Breaking the cast exposes the boss; failure causes a heavy raid-wide impact. Lower boss HP triggers faster, stronger phases. Scripted patterns are not cancelled by ordinary action stun.

Raid · Combat trial

This is a combat trial without rewards. Training parties provide nine sample heroes for anyone to try. Your parties also fight as snapshots, without consuming or changing heroes, equipment, inventory or progress. Potions are not used.

The trial pauses when you leave the tab or hide the window. Returning resumes it; refreshing resets it. Review damage, healing, survivors and cast breaks in the results and retry. Live rewards, repeat rules and additional monsters remain undecided.
